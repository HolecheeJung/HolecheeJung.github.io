A Machine-Learning-Based Mobile Auscultation System for Valvular Heart Disease Screening

Abstract

Traditional cardiac auscultation relies heavily on physician experience, which can limit its use as an initial assessment tool. This paper describes a lightweight intelligent auscultation prototype integrating a single-channel electronic stethoscope interface, a WeChat mini-program frontend, and a FastAPI backend. The offline benchmark uses complete denoised recordings, OpenSMILE ComParE_2016 Functionals (6,373 dimensions), recursive feature elimination with cross-validation (RFECV) to reduce the representation to 173 features (97.3% reduction), and linear SVM classification across five categories (AS, MR, MS, MVP, and N). On a held-out file-level partition of a heterogeneous public collection containing 1,000 audio files (200 per class), the RFECV-SVM achieved 97.5% accuracy and 0.9750 macro-F1, compared with 96.50% accuracy and 0.9648 macro-F1 for the full-feature baseline SVM. Exploratory SHAP analysis summarizes model-level feature attribution across MFCC, auditory-spectrum, FFT spectral, energy-envelope, voicing/periodicity, and zero-crossing-rate descriptor groups. The implemented prototype provides an engineering framework for audio input, analysis, and structured model-output presentation; clinical utility, workflow performance, and generalization require further prospective evaluation.

Keywords

Heart sound, intelligent auscultation, valvular heart disease, OpenSMILE, RFECV, SVM.
